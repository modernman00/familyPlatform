### Target Threads:
- *"How do you interview an aging grandparent before their memory fades?"*
- *"Best questions to ask relatives about childhood in the 1940s/50s"*
- *"How to record family recipes when grandma says 'a pinch of this and a handful of that'"*

### Authoritative Discussion Template:
```markdown
**Title/Comment Contribution:**

When chronicling oral history with aging grandparents, the biggest mistake people make is asking broad, abstract questions like *"Tell me about your childhood."* This causes cognitive overload and usually results in short, polite answers like *"Oh, it was fine, we worked hard."*

Instead, use **sensory memory triggers** that bypass semantic memory and tap directly into episodic recall:

1. **Aromas & Kitchen Sounds:** *"What scents woke you up on Saturday mornings in your first house?"* or *"What did the kitchen smell like when your mother was cooking Sunday roast?"*
2. **Tactile Objects:** Have them hold an old wooden spoon, a pocket watch, or sketch the floor plan of their childhood bedroom on a piece of scrap paper. Physical interaction triggers vivid spatial memories.
3. **Micro-Sessions Over Tea:** Never conduct a 2-hour marathon. Elders tire after 25 minutes. Conduct four 15-minute recordings spaced across a weekend.
4. **Preserve Dialect & Pauses:** Do not edit out pauses, chuckles, or mother-tongue idioms—those inflections carry the emotional DNA of your lineage.

https://myfamilyplatform.com/prompts/questions-to-ask-grandmother-about-childhood
```

## 2. Subreddit: `r/UKPersonalFinance` (LoanEasyFinance & ScoreTenant)

### Target Threads:
- *"Debt Avalanche vs Debt Snowball: which is better?"*
- *"Can I afford £1,600 rent on £45k salary in London?"*
- *"How do letting agents calculate rent affordability? (30x rule question)"*

### Authoritative Discussion Template (Rent Affordability):
```markdown
**Title/Comment Contribution:**

In the UK, letting agencies (HomeLet, Goodlord, Rightmove referencing) strictly enforce the **30x gross annual income rule**:

$$\text{Minimum Gross Annual Income} = \text{Monthly Rent} \times 30$$

For example, on £1,600/month rent:
- Required gross income: $£1,600 \times 30 = £48,000/\text{year}$.
- On a £45,000 salary, your rent represents $42.7\%$ of gross income (or roughly $56\%$ of take-home pay after tax, student loan, and NI).
- In referencing terms, you fall slightly below the 30x threshold (£45k vs £48k). While private landlords might accept this with savings proof, corporate letting agents will typically require either:
  1. A UK-based guarantor earning at least **36x monthly rent** ($£1,600 \times 36 = £57,600/\text{year}$).
  2. Advance rent (often 3–6 months upfront).
  3. A joint co-tenant whose income pools with yours.

Always factor in Council Tax (Band C/D is £150–£180/mo) and utilities (£180–£220/mo) before signing a tenancy contract.

If you want to stress-test your exact rent-to-income ratio and disposable income buffer against UK referencing bands:
https://scoretenant.com/tools/rent-affordability-calculator
```

### Authoritative Discussion Template (Debt Repayment):
```markdown
**Title/Comment Contribution:**

Here is the exact mathematical vs psychological trade-off between Avalanche and Snowball:

- **The Avalanche Method (Highest APR First):** Mathematically optimal. Every extra pound is thrown at the highest-interest balance (e.g. 39.9% overdraft or 29.9% store card) while paying minimums on the rest. Minimises total interest paid to banks and yields the earliest mathematical debt-free date.
- **The Snowball Method (Lowest Balance First):** Behaviourally optimal. Paying off a small £400 balance first delivers a quick psychological win and permanently eliminates one monthly bill from your mental load.

**The Hybrid Rule of Thumb:** If you suffer from debt anxiety or have more than 4 separate creditors, start with Snowball for the first 1 or 2 small debts to build momentum, then immediately pivot to Avalanche for high-APR cards.
```

```markdown
Here is the battle-tested catering formula event planners use for a 4–5 hour evening celebration with 100 adult guests:

1. **Total Drinks Rule:** Estimate **4 drinks per guest** across the night ($100 \times 4 = 400\text{ drinks}$).
```
